Existing Lead 14-Day Nurture

Proposed WhatsApp plus email sequence for existing open Pipedrive leads who have not moved to a 1on1. The intent is not to chase; it is to rebuild context, create a reason to re-engage, and move serious leads toward a team-scheduled 1on1.

Audience

Existing open deals that are not in active 1on1 scheduling, have not recently replied, and have enough contact context for WhatsApp or email.

Cadence

7 touchpoints across 14 days. WhatsApp carries the short nudge. Email carries the richer context.

Exit Condition

Stop the sequence when the lead replies, requests a 1on1, opens the Collective Story and submits, or a sales rep marks manual follow-up active.

DAY 1

Re-open with context

Remind them why the conversation exists: this is about fit with a collective, not a generic property enquiry.

WhatsAppHi {{1}}, picking this up again for {{2}}.\n\nIf the collective is still relevant, the right next step is a short 1on1 with the team. We can help you understand fit, access, and participation clearly.
EmailSubject: Continuing the conversation on {{2}}\n\nHi {{1}},\n\nReaching out because your interest in {{2}} is still open with us.\n\nThe useful next step is not a sales call. It is a context call: whether the collective, location, access, and participation model actually fit what you are looking for.\n\nIf it still feels relevant, we can schedule a 1on1 with the team.
DAY 3

Make the decision smaller

Reduce pressure. The ask is not to commit; it is to clarify whether a 1on1 is worth their time.

WhatsAppHi {{1}}, you do not need to decide on {{2}} from messages.\n\nThe first decision is simpler: does it make sense to understand it properly in a 1on1?
EmailSubject: The first decision is smaller\n\nHi {{1}},\n\nFor {{2}}, the first decision is not whether to participate.\n\nIt is whether the place, the long-term model, and the collective structure are worth understanding properly.\n\nThat is what the 1on1 is for.
DAY 5

Bring in the land and access

Shift the lead from abstract interest to grounded evaluation: place, distance, access, and use.

WhatsAppFor {{2}}, most serious questions are about land, access, time, and how often you see yourself engaging with the place.\n\nA 1on1 helps make that practical.
EmailSubject: Land, access, and actual use\n\nHi {{1}},\n\nThe important questions around {{2}} are usually practical:\n\nHow does the place fit into your life? How often would you engage? What does participation mean beyond ownership language?\n\nA 1on1 is where we can make these questions specific to you.
DAY 7

Invite reply, not form fill

This is the midpoint. Ask for a simple response so sales can manually pick up warm leads.

WhatsAppHi {{1}}, should we keep {{2}} open for you?\n\nReply with:\n1. Schedule 1on1\n2. Send context first\n3. Not relevant now
EmailSubject: Should we keep this open?\n\nHi {{1}},\n\nA quick check on {{2}}.\n\nShould we keep this conversation active for you?\n\nYou can reply with one line: schedule a 1on1, send more context first, or not relevant now.

Manual sales checkpoint after Day 7

Any reply must create a sales task. If they say “schedule,” sales should message personally and book the 1on1. If they ask for context, send the Collective Story. If they say not relevant, cleanly mark the deal.

DAY 9

Use social proof carefully

Without hype: serious members take time, read context, and ask sharper questions.

WhatsAppThe better 1on1s for {{2}} usually happen when people come in with context and real questions.\n\nIf you are still exploring, we can help you get there.
EmailSubject: Better questions make better 1on1s\n\nHi {{1}},\n\nThe most useful 1on1 conversations are not rushed.\n\nThey happen when someone has enough context to ask real questions about the place, the collective, and their own role in it.\n\nIf you are still exploring {{2}}, we can help structure that conversation.
DAY 11

Offer the Collective Story route

For colder leads, move them into the same Collective Story path before asking for a 1on1.

WhatsAppIf a 1on1 feels premature, start with the Collective Story for {{2}}.\n\nIt gives the land and location context before you speak to the team.
EmailSubject: Start with the Collective Story\n\nHi {{1}},\n\nIf a 1on1 feels early, the better starting point is the Collective Story for {{2}}.\n\nIt gives you the ground context: location, access, surroundings, and participation structure.\n\nAfter that, you can decide if a 1on1 is worth scheduling.
DAY 14

Close the loop with dignity

Final touch. Give them a clean exit while leaving a clear 1on1 path open.

WhatsAppClosing the loop on {{2}}, {{1}}.\n\nIf this is still alive for you, reply “1on1” and the team will help schedule it.\n\nIf not, we will stop following up for now.
EmailSubject: Closing the loop on {{2}}\n\nHi {{1}},\n\nClosing the loop on your interest in {{2}}.\n\nIf this is still relevant, reply with “1on1” and the team will help schedule the conversation.\n\nIf the timing is not right, no problem. We will stop following up for now.

Operational Rules

Rule Why it matters Sales ownership
Never continue after a reply Automation should not talk over a human conversation. Rep takes over and updates Pipedrive.
Use both WA and email lightly WhatsApp nudges, email explains. Neither should feel like a chase. Rep reviews replies daily.
Move cold leads to Collective Story It gives context without forcing a call too early. Rep sends story or lets automation trigger it.
Mark clear exits Cleaner CRM, better brand experience, no repeated unwanted follow-ups. Rep marks not relevant / nurture later.
